## Optimization leads

(Leads only — nothing adopted without owner approval. Our live bottlenecks: T5's per-forward re-quantization overhead measured at 2.5–3.2× with the goal ~1.3× after code caching; the int-code exact path measured **slower** than fake-quant; T1's 2.48–3.18× / 2.5–2.8× minimal-code slowdown; T6 probe machinery; G0's K=1024 single-seed +0.28-nat event.)

**1. Our "int-code exact path is slower than fake-quant" measurement now has a theory that tells us where the cost is [verified 2026-11-01, abstract read].** `arXiv:2609.00363` — *Deterministic LLM Inference Across GPU Kernels: Power-of-Two INT8 Quantization Scales and the Limits of Tolerance-Based Conformance* (Teng-Ruei Chen; Aug 25, 2026), with companion `arXiv:2608.13756` v2 (v2 corrects the product-bound attribution: the weight side is exact, not the activation side). Two directly usable results:
  - **The integer alibi.** For shared INT8 operands under a verified no-overflow bound, the **INT32 dot product is exact and order-independent**, so the accumulator cannot be the source of any cross-implementation difference; divergence is localized to **scale application and output rounding *after* the exact accumulator**, and is bounded by one bf16 spacing. Fault injection over 5,880 cells finds every one of five epilogue faults (scale precision, double rounding, multiplication order, output truncation, fused ordering) moves the output by **at most a single bf16 spacing**.
  - **Power-of-two scales make the epilogue a bit-shift and make kernels agree bitwise.** Requantizing every weight scale to its nearest power of two makes CUTLASS and Triton **agree bitwise at 196/196 and 252/252 linear layers** (vs 8/196 and 10/252 under the checkpoints' own scales) and yields byte-identical generated token sequences at 1.7B/8B/14B (8/8 prompts vs 0/8). Cost: perplexity point estimates +0.32% / −0.28% / +0.48%, with the 90% intervals covering zero at the two smaller sizes but **not** at 14B (+0.71% / +0.76%). An earlier reported +157% perplexity for this intervention was an artifact of rewriting scales without requantizing weights (99.8% of it attributable to the weight–scale mismatch).
  **Why this matters for T5 (lead, not adopted):** if our exact int8/ternary path is slower than fake-quant while the *matmul* is exact and fast, the cost is in the epilogue/plumbing — which is exactly the noise-fork's "naive quantize/dequantize plumbing dominates; fused kernels would recover it." Power-of-two (or near-PoT) scales are the concrete, cited way to make that epilogue nearly free *and* to get the bitwise reproducibility our T6 credit-check probes need (a probe that compares our int path against simulation needs the scales to be exactly representable). Caveat to check before adopting: the 14B perplexity-interval result says PoT scales are not free at scale, and our latents are fp32 with per-tensor scales — the interaction with a *per-token* activation quantizer (A8/A4/A2) is not covered by that paper.

- **One identifier resolved, and it changes how we should describe our G0 anomaly.** The bare ACL ID in the G0 writeup — `2025.emnlp-main.1675` — resolves to [verified 2026-11-01, ACL Anthology page fetched]: **Fehlauer, Mahowald, Pimentel. "Convergence and Divergence of Language Models under Different Random Seeds." EMNLP 2025, pages 32982–32991, Suzhou. ACL. DOI 10.18653/v1/2025.emnlp-main.1675; bibkey `fehlauer-etal-2025-convergence`.** It measures convergence as expected per-token KL divergence across seeds and reports: **(i) a four-phase pattern** — uniform → sharp-convergence → sharp-divergence → slow-reconvergence; **(ii) larger models reconverge faster in later training, while smaller models never actually reconverge**, suggesting a minimum model size for stable learned distributions; **(iii) convergence is uneven across linguistic categories** — frequent tokens and function words converge faster and more reliably than infrequent tokens and content words.
  **This is the highest-value verification output of this run.** We sit at 7.3M/37.7M params and 1M/10M tokens — squarely in the regime the paper identifies as *non-reconverging*. So our G0 K=1024 single-seed +0.28-nat event is not merely "known ZO seed instability"; it is **a predicted phenomenon of small-scale LM training that our estimator's credit noise may amplify**, and the paper gives us the acceptance criterion: a cross-seed KL distribution, not a single seed. Concretely, before Tier B we should (a) cite Fehlauer et al. by name rather than by bare anthology ID, (b) report ≥3 seeds per cell for every Tier-B number (the paper's own framing makes single-seed reports indefensible at our scale), and (c) if we want an analogous internal diagnostic, per-token KL across seeds is the cheap one and it is exactly what the four-phase curve is made of.
