## Optimization leads

(For our measured bottlenecks: 2.48–3.18× fp overhead from per-forward re-quantization in `QLinear.effective_weight` [OPEN_QUESTIONS 25], and the ~2.5–2.8× minimal-training-loop slowdown vs plan estimates [T1]. Leads only — nothing adopted without owner approval.)

1. **Cache quantized codes per optimizer step + integer/ternary matmul (the T5 plan).** Citable support that the fast path is real:
   - **EGGROLL, Appendix G.4** [full text read, 2026-10-16]: their pure-integer training runs matmuls as int8 tensor-core ops with int32 accumulation and scaling; they state that on H100 "int8 is the fastest datatype and int8 matrix multiplication with int32 accumulation is the fastest tensor core operation", and they keep all weights int8 / activations integer, "never casting to floating point at any point during training". Direct precedent that a cached-integer forward can be the fast path, not a slowdown. https://arxiv.org/html/2511.16652v2 (§6.1, App. G/H).
   - **T-MAC** (arXiv:2407.00088, EuroSys 2025, DOI 10.1145/3689031.3696099, Microsoft): LUT-based mpGEMM for low-bit weights **without dequantization**; up to 4× throughput vs llama.cpp; basis of bitnet.cpp-style I2_S kernels. Applicable to our w1.58 cached-code matmul. https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.00088
   - **SSR** (arXiv:2610.08403, DATE 2026) and its predecessors RSR/RSR++ (cited therein): GPU ternary-GEMM kernels, 2.1–11.3× over RSR++ (45–95% sparsity), 3.5–6.3× end-to-end on Llama-3 1B inference. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08403
2. **INT8 matmul on Ada (our L4 is SM89).**
   - **gemm-int8** (IST-DASLab, https://github.com/IST-DASLab/gemm-int8): CUTLASS INT8 GEMM PyTorch CUDA extension "specifically optimized for ... Ada Lovelace and Hopper" (SM 8.9–10.0), reporting up to 4× speedup over BF16 matmul on RTX 4090, torch.compile-compatible (no autograd — fine for our forward-only estimator), tuned for transformer dimensions; from the HALO low-precision-training line. [verified via repo page]
   - PyTorch tracks GPU int8 matmul in **pytorch/pytorch#29961**; the underlying primitive is `torch._int_mm`. The `torch._int_mm` docs page did not render for our fetcher this run [unverified detail — confirm the op signature on the Colab before relying on it]. The public **GPU MODE "Advanced Quantization" lecture notes** document `config.force_fuse_int_mm_with_mul = True` (TorchInductor `tuned_fused_int_mm_mul`, torch/_inductor/kernel/mm.py), fusing the int_mm rescale and avoiding materialized INT32 intermediates (~14% kernel-level gain). https://github.com/pytorch/pytorch/issues/29961
3. **torch.compile for the ~2.5–2.8× loop slowdown.**
   - **Ansel et al., "PyTorch 2: Faster Machine Learning Through Dynamic Python Bytecode Transformation and Graph Compilation", ASPLOS 2024** (DOI 10.1145/3620665.3640366; PDF at docs.pytorch.org/assets/pytorch2-2.pdf) — the torch.compile reference.
   - **GraphMend** (arXiv:2509.16248, CGO 2027): 13.8% of 195 surveyed HF models exhibit FX graph breaks; breaks force eager fallback with costly CPU–GPU synchronization; eliminating them gave up to 1.39× steady-state forward speedup. Our hook-heavy Dust loop (pre-hooks, output hooks, `index_add_`) is a plausible graph-break source — profile with `TORCH_LOGS="graph_breaks"` before/after wrapping the inner forward. https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.16248
   - The **Noise repo's speedrun rules** explicitly allow an untimed kernel-compilation warmup — i.e., Q Labs treat compiled-kernel warmup as standard practice for exactly this workload.
4. **SDPA dense-mask costs.** [verified: PyTorch 2.14 docs for `torch.nn.functional.scaled_dot_product_attention`] Three backends (FlashAttention-2 [arXiv:2307.08691], memory-efficient, math); "each of the fused kernels has specific input limitations", and the math reference materializes an (L,S) `attn_bias` added to the scores. Upstream dust.py passes an **explicit mask** to SDPA (OPEN_QUESTIONS 8) — if that mask is exactly causal, `is_causal=True` should let SDPA pick the flash path and avoid the score-shaped bias materialization. Gated on a numerics-parity check (docs warn outputs differ across backends) and on the cuDNN-nondeterminism caveat in the same doc (relevant to our bitwise-resume tests). https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/2.14/generated/torch.nn.functional.scaled_dot_product_attention.html

## Verification

1. **EGGROLL's integer-datatype experiment — RESOLVED [full text read, 2026-10-16, https://arxiv.org/html/2511.16652v2].** Section 6.1 ("Pure Integer Language Model Pretraining") + Appendix G/H confirm: from-scratch **character-level pretraining on minipile** of "EGG" (Evolved Generative GRU; a minGRU variant, 6L-256D, **no activation functions** — nonlinearity comes from int8 saturated-addition clipping, per Foerster 2017), with **all weights in int8 and all activations in integer formats, "never casting to floating point at any point during training"**, trained by EGGROLL (weight-space low-rank ES) at populations up to 2^20 = 1,048,576, batch 16 sequences, truncated ES. Best test loss **3.40 bits/byte**, beating a fp32 6L-256D backprop-SGD *transformer*'s 3.58 bits/byte at the same data batch — at ~180× the GPU-hours; population 2 (MeZO-like) fails badly. Section 6.4/App. K is a separate int8 RWKV-7 fine-tuning via EGGROLL+Adam distillation.
   **Consequence (claim discipline):** "a backprop-free method can pretrain a quantized LM from scratch" is **taken** — by weight-space ES on a recurrent int8 model. Our surviving differentiators: activation-space (virtual-population) perturbation, transformer architecture, matched-budget **pure-ZO vs STE vs smoothed-STE** comparison (EGGROLL ran no STE/QAT baseline on EGG), and modest-population regimes (they needed up to 2^20 members and 180× backprop compute). related_work.md §9/§10/§11.2 must be updated accordingly by the owner.
2. **ZO-Act — RESOLVED** [verified, arXiv:2607.01125, abstract via arXiv listing]: authors **Xun Dong, Yibo Xu, Naigang Wang, Xin Li, Penghang Yin, Zi Yang**. Activation-INFORMED low-rank *weight-subspace* ZO fine-tuning (perturbs lightweight coefficient matrices, not activations); frozen low-bit weights make it compatible with INT4 quantized LLM fine-tuning; fine-tuning only. Authors now captured; no methodological overlap; disambiguate the name in our paper.
