## Optimization leads

(Leads only — nothing adopted without owner approval. Our live bottlenecks: T5's per-forward re-quantization overhead measured at 2.5–3.2× with the goal ~1.3× after code caching; T1's 2.48–3.18× / 2.5–2.8× minimal-code slowdown; T6 probe machinery; G0's K=1024 single-seed +0.28-nat event.)

1. **Independent, public corroboration of our T5 overhead finding, + the exact fix direction** [verified 2026-10-31]. The `quant-int8-relay` entry reports *"int8 tensor-core GEMMs are 3–10× faster raw, but **naive quantize/dequantize plumbing dominates**; fused kernels would recover it"*, and measured the deployed-inference penalty as 3× (qat 17.5 ms vs int 47.3 ms per batch-of-4). This is an external, on-the-same-model confirmation that (a) our measured 2.5–3.2× per-forward re-quantization overhead is the expected magnitude, not an artifact of our implementation, and (b) the win comes from **fusing the quantize/scale into the GEMM epilogue / caching the codes per optimizer step** — exactly our T5 plan (integer-code matmul + code cache). **It also gives us a directly comparable public number to cite** for "quantized forward at the Dust architecture costs ~3× until codes are cached."
2. **T6 STE-bias probe machinery is now public, tested code** [verified 2026-10-31, README + write-up]. `github.com/saanviiyer/apical-dust`: `probe.py` (per-step cosine-to-backprop vs population on a backprop checkpoint) and `bias.py` / `bias_dust.py` (the **Averaging Test**: fix one batch, average N = 1…4096 independent estimates, cosine of the mean against the exact gradient; plateau height = bias, and per-tensor plateaus localize *where* the bias lives). Their measurement on their own Dust reimplementation (embeddings 0.71 plateau vs 0.98 for q/attn/mlp) is a **directly transferable protocol for our STE-bias probe**: run the Averaging Test on *our* estimator at the quantized point and read the per-tensor plateaus — the expected story is that the int8 lattice adds bias on top of the estimator's. Two further citable facts from the same write-up: single-batch cosine is a *biased-vs-noisy-confounded* metric (their 0.26→0.78 improvement trained 0.044 nats worse), and the **SGD-vs-Adam asymmetry** (gap 0.051 with SGD vs 0.398 with Adam) — both directly relevant to how we score our BP-SSTE control and to matching the community's reporting standard.
3. **L4/Ada SM89 low-bit GEMM: a primary source now exists** [verified 2026-10-31, abstract read]. `arXiv:2608.10103`: hand-written PTX beats WMMA by **1.4–1.8× at INT8** and **2.9–4.3× at INT4** on exactly our L4 (Ada SM89), with `mma.sync.m16n8k64.s4` native INT4; and the diagnostic that **coalescing/DRAM cycles, not occupancy or Tensor-Core utilization, predict throughput**. Alongside our logged set (T-MAC 2407.00088, SSR 2610.08403, gemm-int8, EGGROLL int8 fast path), this is the newest SM89-specific datapoint. Corollary from `arXiv:2608.11693`: on B300 there is **no native INT8 tensor-core path at all** (PTX/CUTLASS/vLLM/SGLang all withdraw it) — if we ever benchmark int8 on the Noise-class B300 hardware, expect to be routed to fp8/bf16 or a Triton path.
