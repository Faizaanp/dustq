## ★ Scope 4 — the OPEN_QUESTIONS 40 fairness check: **A DIRECT PRECEDENT EXISTS** [verified 2026-11-01, full text read]

**`arXiv:2605.25469v1` — "JacQuant: STE-Free Quantization-Aware Training via Learned Jacobian Surrogates", Kai Yi, Vignesh Vivekraja, Harshit Khaitan, Steven Li (Meta AI), submitted 25 May 2026, CC BY 4.0, no venue listed.** It replaces STE's identity Jacobian with a learned diagonal/block-diagonal sensitivity map `B(W)` while **keeping the deployed hard quantizer in the forward pass**, at ≤2-bit W and A8/A16, on LLaMA3-1B/3B, Qwen3-1.7B and an 8B W2A16 trajectory.

It states the exact fairness discipline we are deciding on, **three times, verbatim** [arXiv HTML v1, lines verified directly, not paraphrased]:

- §5 **Experimental setup**: "*All methods share identical model architectures, training data, optimization settings, and forward quantization, and differ only in their backward gradient rules. Scale/clipping handling is kept exactly the same as in the corresponding base QAT pipeline; JacQuant adds only the learned backward sensitivity map B(W) and does not introduce a separate scale/clipping advantage.*"
- §5 ablations: "*All methods use identical forward quantization Q(·); only the backward gradient rule differs.*" and "*All baselines use identical model architectures, training data, optimization settings, and quantization configurations.*"
- §1/§5 framing: "*JacQuant is a drop-in backward-rule replacement: the forward quantizer, model, data, and optimizer remain unchanged*", and the contribution bullet "*…while keeping the hard forward quantizer unchanged*" / "*while isolating the algorithmic contribution to the backward rule.*"

**What this means for our decision (OPEN_QUESTIONS 40):** the precedent is in the right family and at the right bit-widths, and it is citable. **Do not adopt it wholesale** — it is a *first-order* paper (it explicitly positions ZeroQAT/FOGZO as changing the *estimation* mechanism and itself as changing only the *backward rule*), so it does not by itself license "one shared forward for ZO, backprop+STE, and backprop+smoothed-STE". But it is a strong citation for the *methods-section sentence* that we deliberately made the quantized forward a controlled variable, and it validates the exact design our owner was leaning toward in #40 ("set the Dust forward mode to `fake` in the configs for all training runs — identical function, ~1.4× faster; keep the exact paths as correctness artifacts + one optional ablation"). Three further things from the same paper are directly useful:

1. **Its theory gives our Gaussian-smoothed-STE control a second, independent anchor.** JacQuant defines its target gradient via **subtractive dithering**: `m(W) = E_r[Q_Δ(W+r) − r]`, `J(W) = ∇_W m(W)`, and calls `g† = J·v̄` "**the gradient of the dither-averaged quantized objective**". That is mathematically the same object ZeroQAT's Appendix G describes (ZO is unbiased for a *smoothed* quantized objective) and the same conceptual target our BP-SSTE control approximates. **Cite JacQuant alongside ZeroQAT App. G when motivating the control.**
2. **It hands a reviewer's likely objection, pre-answered.** Its §C.2 states "*Why we do not use per-step zeroth-order correction: finite-difference corrections typically require additional forward evaluations and must be repeated frequently to remain accurate as weights drift*", and contrasts itself with FOGZO on exactly this. If a TMLR reviewer asks why our ZO arm pays a per-step query cost, we need a one-line cost-accounting answer (we report wall-clock per update alongside loss; the fork `quant-int8-relay`'s 3–10×-raw-int8-GEMM-but-plumbing-dominated measurement is the empirical peg).
3. **Its mechanistic diagnostic is a ready-made probe idea**: finite-difference `Ĵᵢ = (Q(w+ε) − Q(w−ε))/2ε` used only as a *diagnostic reference* to score each training rule's coordinate-wise gradient error — directly comparable to our T6 STE-bias probe and to the fork's `tests/check_quant.py` cosine harness.

**Weaker, partial precedent (for completeness):** **`arXiv:2605.10673`** (CAQ-ZO) improves "the trained NF4 baseline **under the same quantizer and evaluation budget**" — matched-quantizer fairness, but ZO-vs-ZO, not ZO-vs-STE. **So: a fairness precedent for "share one quantized forward across gradient rules" exists (JacQuant); one for "share it across a ZO estimator and two STE controls at matched from-scratch budgets" does not — our methods-section text should claim the former as precedent and the latter as our contribution.**
